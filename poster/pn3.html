Why AWGN-Trained Weights Fail on Fading

Reused on a fading channel without retraining, the AWGN weights give a BER that increases with SNR. A real system should not behave this way, so we kept every weight fixed and changed one thing at a time:

  • Clamping the network outputs changed the BER by less than 4%, and the curve still increased.
  • Removing the symbols in deep fades made the BER worse above 10 dB.
  • Clamping the noise value passed to the demapper into the range it saw during training restored a decreasing curve, without changing any weight.

The cause is on the input side. At 18 dB, 89% of symbols have an effective noise below anything seen in training, against 4.6% in deep fades. Retraining gives 1e-3 at 13.2 dB and 1e-4 at 14.9 dB.

Training Without a Channel Model

All the results above used backpropagation through a differentiable channel model. This may not be feasible in practice, where the channel acts as a black box and only its inputs and outputs can be observed. There is then no channel layer connecting the two neural networks at the transmitter and the receiver, because the channel is not differentiable.

Instead we feed the training loss from the receiver back to the transmitter and use it to train the transmitter by reinforcement learning [4]. The two networks are still jointly optimised, without requiring a channel model.

  • Removing the channel model costs 0.12, 0.14 and 0.13 dB at BER 1e-2, 1e-3 and 1e-4.
  • The reinforcement-learned system is still 0.23 to 0.25 dB better than classical 64-QAM.
  • The two training methods place each symbol at almost the same position, so reinforcement learning converges to a similar constellation.

Conclusion

On AWGN, end-to-end learning gives a gain of 0.37 dB. Under Rayleigh fading with estimated CSI the gain is small: 0.01 and 0.11 dB at 1e-2 and 1e-3. The end-to-end system can also be trained with no channel model, at a cost of 0.12 to 0.14 dB.

Next Steps

  • Deploy on real hardware, such as a software-defined radio, and measure whether the gain still appears outside simulation.
  • Meta-learning for fast adaptation [3]. The section above shows that the receiver fails when its input distribution changes.
  • Extend to OFDM and multipath [2], where a convolutional demapper can use the correlation between neighbouring subcarriers.
  • Replace zero-forcing with MMSE equalisation, which keeps the effective noise bounded.
  • Improve performance under fading. We will continue to improve the proposed system on Rayleigh and other realistic channel models.
